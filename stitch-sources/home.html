Where Hazards Collide

thermostat

Extreme Heat

Wet-bulb temperatures are nearing the threshold of human survivability in the Indo-Gangetic plain and Sahel. Heat days above 40°C are rising by an average of 4.2 days per decade, directly impacting cognitive labor and agricultural yield.

air

Toxic Air

Particulate matter (PM₂.₅) levels consistently exceed WHO guidelines by 15-20 times in critical growth zones. This environmental toxicity creates a "biological tax" on developing populations, reducing life expectancy and cognitive capacity.

groups

Demographic Pressure

The highest fertility rates globally are now concentrated in high-intensity hazard zones. This creates an compounding pressure where the infrastructure for education and health is perpetually outpaced by population growth.
